Separating age from mileage

  
Fitted once on every used listing with a price, a year and an odometer reading — the filters at the top of the page do not change it. In this market model year and odometer are  correlated — older cars have simply been driven more — so a median-by-year curve blends the two and calls the total “depreciation”. This fits both at once (restricted cubic splines on age and on log-mileage, against log price, with per-model slopes), which lets each be held still while the other moves.

Premiums and spreads, once age and mileage are held equal

  
Each row is the price difference for two otherwise identical vehicles — same model, same age, same odometer. Bars crossing the zero line are differences the data cannot distinguish from nothing.

  

  

  




Asking prices, not transaction prices — a dealer’s listed number is the top of the negotiation, and the spread between listed and sold is not visible in this data. “Depreciation” here is cross-sectional: it compares different cars of different ages listed on the same day, not one car followed over time, so it also absorbs trim mix, model redesigns and the used-market conditions of a single snapshot.
